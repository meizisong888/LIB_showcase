AI-Assisted Transcription of Historical Library Documents

A scan preserves the page. A transcription helps us find what is written on it.

AI can turn difficult-to-read historical handwriting into readable draft transcriptions, helping readers understand the content and giving library staff a starting point for review.

For an initial reading, a draft can help readers understand the general content. For accurate quotation, cataloging, and search, names, dates, places, and other important details still need checking against the scan.

These samples have existing public transcriptions, which allow us to compare the AI’s first-pass output with a reference. The intended application is to assist with documents that do not yet have transcriptions. Here, the existing texts let us test and demonstrate AI reading, rather than repeat completed transcription work.

The AI’s first pass did not read the references. They were used only afterward for comparison and assisted correction, and can contain errors. Reference-assisted corrections do not show what AI would achieve independently on documents without references.

What the numbers mean

Readable output still needs checking.

In these three examples, AI transcribed much of the handwritten content into readable drafts. Comparison with existing public transcriptions revealed both formatting differences and content errors. AI can therefore provide a useful first draft, while human review remains necessary to verify important details.

Some differences in punctuation, spacing, line breaks, and abbreviation forms may not prevent understanding. Others change the content and can affect interpretation and search. In sample 03, the first pass read the ship name Arago as George. Searching that first pass for Arago would miss the page; the checked text uses the reference-assisted correction.

CER and WER compare the first-pass AI output with the public reference, not the checked text. They count character and word edits after Unicode NFC normalization, case folding and whitespace collapse. Punctuation and uncertainty markers remain. They measure disagreement, including transcription conventions; the reference can contain errors. Neither 1 − CER nor 1 − WER is presented as factual accuracy.

No time savings or cost savings are claimed. The original AI text is preserved in full.
